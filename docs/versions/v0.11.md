# Baru Budget v0.11 — Financings

## Status

IN_PROGRESS — architectural analysis approved on 2026-09-07 (`docs/ARCHITECTURE_ANALYSIS_V0.11.md`); implementation complete and verified on the local stack. Hosted migrations and deployment pending.

## Objective

Record financings of assets and follow what is left to pay: asset value, down payment, financed amount, amortization system, instalments generated month by month and outstanding balance, with the asset value never becoming a transaction so acquisition and instalments can never be counted twice. Architecture reference: `docs/ARCHITECTURE_ANALYSIS_V0.11.md`.

## Scope

- Database: `financing_system` enum, `financings` table with a generated `financed_amount`, financing columns on `transactions`, shape constraint, partial unique indexes, `financing_monthly_rate`, `financing_installment_amounts` and `generate_financing_schedule`.
- Two explicit amortization systems with documented, tested formulas: Price and SAC.
- The non-duplication rule of MASTER_PROMPT section 35, enforced by shape rather than by report adjustment.
- Financings page, financing detail with the amortization schedule, financing form with a live preview.
- Financings card on the dashboard; financing mark in the transactions list.
- Amortization math promoted to `shared/finance`, shared by loans and financings.
- pgTAP and Vitest math tests, including the non-duplication tests.

## Technical Behavior

- **The asset value is never a transaction.** Only the down payment and the instalments are recorded, so the total that leaves the pocket is the down payment plus the instalments, which is the asset value plus interest. This is how MASTER_PROMPT section 35 asks for double counting to be prevented, and it is structural: there is no report adjustment to forget.
- **A financing produces no income**, unlike a loan disbursement. The bank pays the seller directly, so no money enters the account and `monthly_transaction_totals` needed no new exception.
- **`financed_amount` is a generated column.** Section 35 asks for the field and section 24 forbids storing what is derivable; a generated column satisfies both and cannot diverge from asset value minus down payment. Writing it by hand is rejected by the database.
- **Two amortization systems, never mixed.** Price keeps the instalment fixed with interest falling and amortization growing; SAC amortizes a constant share of the principal, so the instalment starts higher and falls every month. A zero rate degenerates to splitting the financed amount in both.
- **The yearly rate conversion is always effective** (`(1+a)^(1/12) − 1`) because both systems capitalize over the outstanding balance. Unlike loans there is no convention to choose.
- **Price is implemented once.** In the database `financing_installment_amounts` delegates Price to `loan_installment_amounts`; in the client both features consume `shared/finance/amortization.ts`. A correction to the formula can only be made in one place.
- **Rounding.** Instalments are computed in whole cents and the difference lands on the last one, so the early instalments match what the bank charges and the schedule ends at exactly zero.
- **Three different numbers, all derived.** "Total a pagar" is the sum of unpaid instalments. "Saldo devedor" is the financed amount not yet amortized, taken from the schedule at the last paid instalment. "Total desembolsado" is the down payment plus every instalment. They are shown together because confusing them is the classic mistake of this domain.
- **Generation is idempotent.** `generate_financing_schedule` creates the down payment and the missing instalments with `on conflict do nothing`, guarded by partial unique indexes. A deleted instalment is recreated; a cancelled one keeps its number. It runs with invoker rights, so VIEW cannot generate and MANAGE generates for the owner. A zero down payment never becomes a transaction, and the down payment can be skipped when it was recorded by hand.
- **A transaction belongs to at most one debt**: a constraint refuses a row carrying both a loan and a financing.
- **Deleting the financing keeps the transactions**, clearing the link. The instalments keep their number as history, which is recorded as a known limitation.

## Security Changes

- Schedule generation runs with invoker rights; row level security decides each insert, covered by pgTAP with VIEW and MANAGE.
- Financing references are validated against the owner: account, expense category for the instalments and expense category for the down payment, plus household membership.
- The shape constraint refuses a financing transaction that is not an expense and an instalment number without a financing on a non-expense row.
- `financing_monthly_rate`, `financing_installment_amounts` and `generate_financing_schedule` are not executable by `anon`; `validate_financing_references` is not executable by `authenticated`.
- Verified on the local stack: after generating a schedule the transactions of the financing sum to the down payment plus the instalments, and no transaction carries the asset value.

## Known Limitations

- Monetary correction by index (TR, IPCA, IGP-M) is not modelled; the schedule is nominal.
- Mandatory insurance, administration fees and other monthly charges are not modelled.
- Early amortization, refinancing and grace periods are not modelled.
- Deleting a financing keeps its transactions and clears the link.
- Financing instalments do not appear in the installments commitment page, which covers purchases only.
- The asset itself is not tracked: no depreciation, resale value or settlement on sale.

## Out of Scope

Consortium, leasing, SACRE and bullet systems, monetary correction by index, insurance and fees, early amortization and refinancing, asset tracking and depreciation, selling the asset with settlement, the Excel format which is v0.12.
