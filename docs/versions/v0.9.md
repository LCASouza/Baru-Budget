## Technical Behavior

- **Who paid is the owner; who is responsible is an allocation.** One row per person, in amounts, with no stored percentage. Splitting is available on expenses only.
- **The sum invariant lives in the database.** Deferred constraint triggers on both `transaction_allocations` and `transactions` check at commit that, whenever a transaction has allocations, they add up exactly to its amount. Replacing a whole split passes through intermediate states without tripping, and changing the amount without adjusting the split fails.
- **`set_transaction_allocations` replaces a split in one call** with invoker rights: it deletes the current rows, validates the requested sum eagerly for a clear error and inserts the new ones. An empty list removes the split.
- **A settlement is a transaction of kind SETTLEMENT**, not a separate table, as recorded in the approved analysis. It carries a counterparty and a direction: PAY debits the owner account and RECEIVE credits it, which `account_balances` now reflects. It is never income or expense, so `monthly_transaction_totals` ignores it, and it never touches the expense that created the debt.
- **The counterparty sees what involves them.** Row level security lets the responsible person read the expense they were allocated a share of, the whole split of that expense and the name of who paid; and lets the counterparty of a settlement read it. Accounts, cards and balances of the other person stay out of reach. `is_allocated_to_me` and `shares_ledger_with` are security definer so the policies do not recurse.
- **Balances are always derived.** `people_balances` builds one entry per allocation (the responsible owes the payer) and one per settlement (whoever hands over the money gains credit), keeps the pairs that involve the caller and returns the signed net: positive is receivable, negative is payable. Allocations to the payer themselves and cancelled entries never count.
- The equal split includes the payer as a row of their own and puts the remaining cents on the first person, so the rows always add up to the amount.

## Security Changes

- The split invariant is guaranteed by the database, not by the interface.
- The counterparty gains read access to exactly the transaction that involves them, its split and the name of the payer; nothing else of that owner.
- Only someone who can manage the owner writes allocations; the responsible person cannot rewrite their own share.
- A settlement cannot be created with a category, a card, an instalment, a recurrence link or with oneself as counterparty.
- Every security definer helper, including the trigger functions, has execute revoked from `anon`.
- Verified through PostgREST on the local stack: both sides of a pair see the same balance with opposite signs, the responsible user sees the shared expense but no account of the payer, and `anon` sees nothing.

## Known Limitations

- Splitting and settlements have not been checked in production yet; the behaviour is verified by tests and on the local stack only.
- A settlement is recorded against a person, not against specific items.
- The database accepts an allocation to any profile; the interface only offers household members, grant counterparts and people with an open balance.
- Splitting is available on expenses only.
- The balance uses what the caller can see; a debt only appears once the other side records the expense.

## Out of Scope

Stored percentages, income splitting, settling specific items, reminders and charging, loans and financings.
