# Baru Budget v0.4 — Households and Sharing

## Status

DELIVERED — 2026-09-06. Technical criteria met and deployed; the production walkthrough with two users was deferred by the administrator and remains listed under Known Limitations until performed.

## Objective

Share finances in two distinct ways: households, where people take part in a common financial set, and personal access grants, where a user gives another user VIEW or MANAGE access to their own finances. Every rule is enforced by the database, access is never transitive and managing finances never includes managing security. Architecture reference: `docs/ARCHITECTURE_ANALYSIS_V0.4.md`.

## Technical Behavior

- Authorization is decided by `security definer` functions with `search_path = ''`, executable only by `authenticated`: `can_view(owner)`, `can_manage(owner)`, `is_household_member(h)`, `is_household_admin(h)`, `is_active_member(h, u)`, `shares_household_with(u)`, `is_grant_counterpart(u)`, `category_used_in_my_households(c)`. `can_view` and `can_manage` only consider active grants whose `granted_user_id` is the current user, so received access never propagates.
- Policies: `accounts`, `categories` and `transactions` read through `can_view(owner_user_id)` and write through `can_manage(owner_user_id)`; `transactions` are also readable by active members of `household_id`; `categories` are also readable while used by a transaction of one of the user's households; `profiles` are readable by the user, grant counterparts and household co-members; `households` and `household_members` are readable by active members and written by active administrators; `financial_access_grants` are readable by owner and grantee and written by the owner only.
- A grant gives access to everything owned by the owner (accounts, balances through `account_balances`, categories, transactions). Membership gives access only to transactions tagged with the household; members never see each other's accounts, balances or personal transactions.
- Household transactions keep the owner's account and category. `validate_transaction_references` requires `owner_user_id` to be an active member of `household_id`. Deleting a household sets `household_id` to null on its transactions.
- The creator of a household becomes ADMIN through an `after insert` trigger. `ensure_household_admin` blocks demoting, deactivating or deleting the last active ADMIN, except while the household itself is being deleted. Leaving goes through `leave_household`, which only deactivates the caller's own membership. Re-adding a former member reactivates the row (`upsert` on the primary key).
- Revocation sets `revoked_at`; only rows with `revoked_at is null` give access. A unique partial index allows one active grant per pair; `grants_not_self` blocks self grants.
- `lookup_user_by_email(email_address)` matches the exact e-mail case-insensitively against `auth.users` and returns at most one `(id, display_name)`; it is not executable by `anon`.
- The context is resolved from the persisted key against the loaded options; a missing option (revoked grant, removed household) falls back to the personal context. Stores reload when the context changes because their `resource()` params derive from it.
- In a shared context the data owner is the grant owner; the grantee creates records with `owner_user_id` = owner and the audit triggers record the grantee in `created_by`/`updated_by`. In a household context the data owner is the user; new transactions are tagged with the household by default.
- Audit trigger behaviour on insert: a forged `created_by` is replaced by the authenticated user (households included), so the creator of a household is always the authenticated user.

## Security Changes

- Non-transitive access enforced by the authorization functions and covered by pgTAP (`grants.test.sql`).
- MANAGE cannot create, change or revoke grants, cannot change household membership and cannot edit the owner's profile (owner-only and admin-only policies; pgTAP).
- Household visibility limited to household-tagged transactions and the categories they use; accounts and `account_balances` never gain a household rule (pgTAP `household_transactions.test.sql`).
- Every `security definer` function has execute revoked from `public` and `anon`; `rls_enabled.test.sql` now asserts this for every such function in `public`.
- Revoked grants and inactive memberships lose access immediately (pgTAP).
- Verified through PostgREST on the local stack: a VIEW grantee reads the owner's balances and is refused on insert (`42501`); a household member reads only household-tagged transactions and the categories they use; an unrelated user reads nothing; `anon` cannot call `lookup_user_by_email`.

## Known Limitations

- The production walkthrough with two users (household, VIEW, MANAGE, revocation) has not been performed yet; the behaviour is verified by pgTAP and on the local stack only.
- The dashboard keeps mock data and ignores the context until v0.5.
- Members and grantees are added by exact e-mail lookup; there is no invitation flow.
- Households have no categories or accounts of their own; household transactions use the personal account and category of who registered them.
- A user with MANAGE cannot tag the owner's transactions with the owner's households unless the user is also a member (the field is hidden in a shared context).

## Out of Scope

Dashboard by context and charts, transaction allocations and settlements, invitations with acceptance, household-owned categories or accounts, additional roles, full audit log, notifications.
