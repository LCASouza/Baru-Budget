## Objective

Record installment purchases and follow the future commitment: a purchase in N instalments generates N transactions, each in its own competence (card invoice or account month), with current instalment, remaining ones, next instalment and total committed. Architecture reference: `docs/ARCHITECTURE_ANALYSIS_V0.7.md`.

## Technical Behavior

- **Each instalment is a real transaction.** A purchase of 900 in 3 instalments creates three expenses of 300. The invoices, the dashboard, the category totals and the account balance already handle them without a special case, because each instalment is an ordinary expense of its own month.
- **Split.** `split_installment_amounts` works in whole cents and puts the remainder on the first instalment, so the instalments always add up to the total: 100 in 3 becomes 33.34, 33.33 and 33.33.
- **Competence.** On a card, the first instalment uses `invoice_due_date_for` and each next one falls on the following invoice, with the due day clamped to the month. On an account, each instalment is dated one month after the previous one with the purchase day clamped, so a purchase on the 31st of January produces an instalment on 28 February. The expense date of every instalment also shifts monthly, which keeps the monthly expenses aligned with the invoices.
- **Status.** Card instalments are created as PAID (the purchase happened; the money leaves when the invoice is paid). Account instalments are created as PENDING with a due date, so each one is settled individually and only then moves the account balance.
- **Generation in the database.** `create_installment_purchase` inserts every instalment in one statement with invoker rights: row level security decides each insert exactly as a direct insert would, so VIEW cannot create instalments and MANAGE creates them for the owner, recorded in `created_by`. The group identifier is generated by the database and never comes from the client.
- **Consolidated view.** `installment_purchases` returns one row per group with the total, the recorded count and how much is still ahead of today, using the invoice due date as competence on a card and the transaction date on an account. Cancelled instalments leave the purchase without deleting history.
- Editing one instalment never changes the others. "Excluir parcelamento" removes every instalment of the group, including the ones already recorded.

## Security Changes

- The generation function runs with invoker rights: every insert passes through the policies, which is covered by pgTAP with VIEW and MANAGE.
- The shape constraint refuses instalment numbering without a group, a number above the count, and an income split into instalments.
- The consolidated view runs with the caller privileges, so commitment never exposes transactions the user cannot read.
- `split_installment_amounts`, `shift_month_day` and `create_installment_purchase` are not executable by `anon`.

## Known Limitations

- Installments have not been checked in production yet; the behaviour is verified by tests and on the local stack only.
- An installment purchase can only be created as a whole; editing one instalment never changes the others and there is no bulk edit.
- Interest inside an installment purchase, early settlement and renegotiation are not modelled.
- Splitting instalments between people arrives in v0.9.
- The commitment per month covers the six months ahead of today.

## Out of Scope

Bulk editing of an installment purchase, early settlement with discount, interest, renegotiation, splitting between people, fixed expenses and recurrences, income instalments.
