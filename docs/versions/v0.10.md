# Baru Budget v0.10 — Loans

## Status

IN_PROGRESS — implementation complete locally (build, lint, Vitest and pgTAP passing); hosted migrations and production check pending.

## Objective

Record loans taken and follow what is left to pay: principal, rate, an explicit interest model, instalments generated month by month, outstanding principal and remaining total, with the disbursement increasing cash without inflating the income of the month. Architecture reference: `docs/ARCHITECTURE_ANALYSIS_V0.10.md`.

## Technical Behavior

- **A loan is not income.** The disbursement is an INCOME tied to the loan: it increases the account balance and is excluded from `monthly_transaction_totals` and from the period summaries, which is what MASTER_PROMPT section 34 requires. The instalments are ordinary expenses of their months.
- **The disbursement carries its own income category**, chosen on the loan. When none is chosen, no disbursement is generated and the user records the money themselves. This keeps the transaction shape unchanged: every income still has a category.
- **Two interest models, never mixed.** Simple interest charges `P·i` every month with constant amortization; Price uses `P·i / (1 − (1+i)^−n)` with interest falling and amortization growing. A zero rate degenerates to splitting the principal in both models, handled explicitly so Price never divides by zero.
- **The yearly rate conversion is declared per model:** proportional (`a/12`) for simple interest, effective (`(1+a)^(1/12) − 1`) for Price. Twelve equivalent monthly rates rebuild the yearly rate, which is covered by a test.
- **Rounding.** Instalments are computed in whole cents and the difference lands on the last one, so the early instalments match what the lender charges and the schedule ends at exactly zero.
- **Two different balances, both derived.** "Total a pagar" is the sum of unpaid instalments, principal plus future interest. "Saldo devedor" is the principal not yet amortized, taken from the schedule at the last paid instalment. They are shown side by side because confusing them is the classic mistake of this domain. Nothing is stored, so `outstanding_balance` from section 33 exists as a derived value.
- **Generation is idempotent.** `generate_loan_schedule` creates the disbursement and the missing instalments with `on conflict do nothing`, guarded by partial unique indexes on the disbursement and on each instalment number. A deleted instalment is recreated; a cancelled one keeps its number. It runs with invoker rights, so VIEW cannot generate and MANAGE generates for the owner.
- **Deleting the loan keeps the transactions**, clearing the link. The instalments keep their number as history, and the disbursement goes back to counting as income, which is recorded as a known limitation.
- The detail page compares the schedule with what was actually recorded and flags any instalment whose amount differs, instead of rewriting anything.

## Security Changes

- Schedule generation runs with invoker rights; row level security decides each insert, covered by pgTAP with VIEW and MANAGE.
- Loan references are validated against the owner: account, expense category for the instalments and income category for the disbursement, plus household membership.
- The shape constraint refuses a numbered disbursement, a loan expense without a number and an instalment number without a loan.
- `loan_monthly_rate`, `loan_installment_amounts` and `generate_loan_schedule` are not executable by `anon`.
- Verified on the local stack: the R$ 10.000 disbursement raises the account balance and never appears in the monthly income totals.

## Known Limitations

- Only loans taken are covered; lending money with interest is not modelled.
- Renegotiation, early amortization, grace periods, insurance and fees are not modelled.
- Deleting a loan keeps its transactions and the disbursement goes back to counting as income.
- Loan instalments do not appear in the installments commitment page, which covers purchases only.

## Out of Scope

Loans granted with interest, renegotiation, portability, early amortization, grace periods, insurance and fees, compound interest without instalments, financings.
